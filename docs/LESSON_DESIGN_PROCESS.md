# Designing connected lessons

Start with the supplied standards, not a fixed screen template. Group standards only when their concepts support one central question and a coherent learning journey. Preserve their IDs and all evidence levels. A group is a lesson; a standard remains an assessment target.

## The authoring pass

1. Name the central question, prerequisite knowledge, likely misunderstandings, and the independent performance expected for each standard.
2. Map every standard to a worked demonstration (Show me), supported practice (Guide me), and a fresh application (Let me try). A passing mention, opened resource, or completed video is not evidence of comprehension.
3. Choose the teaching form for the concept: a scene to investigate, a manuscript to examine, a Scripture comparison, a consequence map, a branching conversation, a claim clinic, or a sequence to construct. Explain why this form helps. Do not assign forms by rotating standards through templates.
4. Design the attention moment before selecting media. An image must reveal something to notice; a video needs a before-viewing question and a specific after-viewing task; a game must exercise the actual distinction being learned. Show captions/transcripts and keyboard alternatives. Never require speed or dragging to demonstrate knowledge.
5. Give each incorrect response a diagnosis and a useful next teaching move. Guided correction can reuse the task. Independent reassessment must establish the reasoning; do not credit a revealed answer as independent mastery.
6. Revisit an earlier concept in a new context later in the same lesson and name where it should return in a subsequent lesson. Reuse a resource only when the second use asks the learner to see something new.
7. Compare the proposed lesson with its two neighbors: opening, main activity, question form, media and closing challenge. If the sequence repeats, reconsider the teaching design. Keep navigation consistent while varying intellectual work.
8. Review content against the uploaded Apostolic standards. Represent other beliefs accurately. Review the exact external excerpt, source identity, permitted use, playback, captions and outbound controls. Keep source credit in-app. Do not add unreviewed content merely to satisfy a media quota.
9. Exercise wrong, partial, supported and independent responses, interruption/resume, keyboard use and narrow layouts. Distinguish automated flow checks from live assessment and visual inspection.

## OG.1 first connected lesson

Central question: Does the confession of one God change as Scripture unfolds?

| Standard | Show me | Guide me | Let me try | Reinforcement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OG.1.1.18 | Illustrated confession, phrase explanation, context | Rebuild the phrase meanings | Explain and recall the Shema; address the compound-unity argument | Reappears in NT continuity and final explanation |
| OG.1.2.18 | Before/beside/after visual and six Isaiah witnesses | Match rival claims to decisive evidence | Recall four declarations and answer an objection | Isaiah frames the closing doctrinal response |
| OG.1.3.18 | Illustrated OT → Jesus → apostles sequence | Construct the argument’s order | Explain the contribution of the NT witnesses | Compare with the initial confession |
| OG.1.4.18 | Fair side-by-side doctrinal comparison and incarnation | Classify accurate claims and misrepresentations | Explain both positions and respond to Jesus’ prayer | Retrieve the Shema and Isaiah in the answer |

| OG.1.5.18 | Worship/ethics/mission exploration | Decide how to respond to fictional dishonesty | Connect the confession to concrete faithful responses | Return to Deuteronomy 6:5 and James 2:19 |

The lesson teaches and guides all five connected concepts before the independent synthesis tasks. The grouped lesson has its own saved state. Existing individual lessons and saves are preserved in the registry but the main learning path displays the group once. Only independently established rubric evidence populates its five-goal ledger. Supported responses remain practice.

Current boundaries: this implementation includes original vector illustrations and an optional timed illustrated sequence, not a video. Four guide activities use structured connections with different presentation and cognitive tasks; future lesson designs should also use other interaction families. No new external video has been inserted. Live AI grading and real-browser visual testing remain separate release checks; deterministic tests do not certify either. Other domains have not yet undergone this authoring pass.

Source reinforcement reviewed for OG.1.4: UPCI Our Beliefs (https://upci.org/our-beliefs/) and The True Humanity of Jesus Christ (https://upci.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/The_True_Humanity_of_Jesus_Christ.pdf), reviewed October 2, 2026 for the narrow claims of one God’s revelation and genuine incarnation. Source URLs are authoring metadata, not learner navigation.
