## 1. Authoritative learning contract

**Exact standard statement:**

> The student can identify, quote from memory, and theologically interpret the major monotheistic declarations of Isaiah (43:10, 44:6, 44:8, 45:5–6, 45:21–22, 46:9), demonstrating that God's exclusive identity is asserted with language that admits no plurality of divine persons.

**Source scope:** Isaiah 40–46 constitutes the most concentrated monotheistic argument in the Old Testament. The student must understand that these declarations are not merely poetic — they are theological propositions, made in the context of Israel's temptation toward polytheism, using the most absolute language available. The student must be able to show that "beside me there is no God" and "before me there was no God formed" close off any interpretation that posits a second or third divine person alongside the Father.

**Source instructional focus:** Walk students through Isaiah 40–46 as a sustained argument, not a verse list. Show the courtroom imagery of Isaiah 43 — God calls the nations to produce a witness, and none can. Let the cumulative weight of these declarations land before applying them polemically.

**Exact Evidence of Learning levels** (these are *standard-defined* performance levels, not synonymous with Depth of Knowledge):

| Level | Standard label | Source evidence text |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recognition | Can locate the major passages and state their general claim. |
| 2 | Explanation | Quotes at least four passages and explains the force of each declaration — no God before, beside, or after. |
| 3 | Application | Shows how the Isaiah declarations function as the background for New Testament monotheism; applies them to refute the concept of eternal divine persons alongside each other. |
| 4 | Defense | Responds to the trinitarian reading that these texts are about God's uniqueness among other gods (not internal structure), demonstrating that the grammatical force of "beside me there is no God" cannot be qualified to mean "no other divine species" while still permitting multiple co- equal persons of one God. |

## 2. Learner promise and conceptual boundaries

**Learner-facing destination:** “I can assemble Isaiah’s declarations into a connected argument about the LORD’s exclusive identity, quote key passages from memory, and explain how this witness informs later Scripture.”

**Prerequisites:** OG.1.1.18's Shema; distinguish poetic imagery from the theological claims Isaiah explicitly makes. Do not assume prior expertise in prophetic literature.

**Required understandings:**
- Isaiah 43–46 is presented as a connected prophetic challenge to idols and rival claims—not a collection of isolated slogans.
- God speaks as the sole LORD; Isaiah uses before / beside / after and first / last / none else to describe divine exclusivity.
- Isaiah 43:10, 44:6, 44:8, 45:5–6, 45:21–22, and 46:9 each contribute a distinct or reinforcing assertion.
- The Apostolic/Oneness interpretation sees these statements as consistent with the absolute singularity confessed in Deuteronomy 6:4.
- Other Christians sometimes read these verses as excluding rival gods while allowing three eternal divine persons; learners should understand the distinction between what the passage expressly asserts and the doctrinal argument built from it.

**Standard boundary:** Build the evidence needed here without absorbing the teaching and assessment obligations of adjoining standards. The **Apostolic interpretation** is the curriculum's doctrinal conclusion; competing interpretations should be represented accurately before evaluating them with Scripture. Unsupported claims must not be attributed directly to a biblical writer.

## 3. Scripture and reasoning sequence

| Order | KJV passage | Instructional contribution | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Isaiah 43:8–13, especially 43:10 | Set the witness/courtroom context; distinguish the LORD from gods made or alleged. | Do not imply the book is a literal photograph of an ancient trial. |
| 2 | Isaiah 44:6–8 | Follow 'first and last' and 'beside me there is no God'; connect to the rhetorical question of a rival. | Read 44:8 with 44:6, not as unrelated poetry. |
| 3 | Isaiah 45:5–6, 21–22 | Extend the claim: no other LORD; the same one God calls the ends of the earth to salvation. | Do not reduce the passage to a private national slogan. |
| 4 | Isaiah 46:8–10, especially 46:9 | God calls for remembrance and contrasts himself with fabricated idols. | Keep the immediate address and comparison clear. |
| 5 | Deuteronomy 6:4; Mark 12:29 | Re-anchor the prophetic witness and preview the New Testament continuity. | Detailed NT synthesis is OG.1.3.18. |

Required texts must be available for actual reading; generated media and optional explanations cannot replace a biblical passage.

## 4. Diagnostic misconceptions and correction

| Observed misconception | Teaching response | Independent check |
|---|---|---|
| 'Isaiah is just repeating the same verse.' | Ask learners to sort what each passage adds: temporal exclusion, beside, identity, universal call. | Can the student cite two different statements and explain the different contribution? |
| 'These are only attacks on wooden idols; they say nothing about the identity of God.' | Teach the immediate idol contrast and then read the LORD's own exclusive first-person declarations. | Can they describe both the historical contrast and the doctrinal claim? |
| 'Each statement by itself proves every later theological issue.' | Explain the difference between a source's explicit claim and a doctrinal interpretation of its implications. | Can they state the claim before applying it? |
| 'A verse list equals an argument.' | Have them order passages by the contribution of each and narrate the reasoning. | Do they link passages using causal/explanatory transitions? |

## 5. Lesson experience: seven different thinking tasks

| Beat | Student action | Scripture / teacher move | Proposed interaction | Evidence collected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Witness summons | Observe who is addressed and asked to testify. | Isaiah 43:8–10; short historical context. | A voiced, Scripture-centered scene of people hearing prophetic testimony; no theatrical invented dialogue. | Accurate identification of speaker/claim. |
| 2. Before / beside / after | Identify which statement excludes which rival claim. | Isaiah 43:10; 44:6; 45:5. | Interactive evidence board: match claim → specific passage, with passage visible. | Reason for each match, not just correctness. |
| 3. Build cumulative force | Explain how 44:8, 45:21–22, and 46:9 strengthen the case. | Read three additional passages with inline context. | Animated but optional evidence layering, then an accessible static list. | One sentence on each passage's contribution. |
| 4. Memory retrieval | Quote at least four major declarations from memory, then verify against KJV. | Offer cues before unaided recall; no punishing typos. | Text or oral recall, with reference selection and KJV comparison. | Accurate verse content and attribution. |
| 5. Reasoning challenge | Evaluate a claim that exclusivity only means 'no rival species of god.' | State an alternate reading fairly; apply the Oneness reasoning to Isaiah in context. | Argument-building interaction, not caricatured debate. | Independent explanation with scriptural support. |
| 6. Bridge to Jesus | State what must remain true when moving to Mark 12:29. | Reintroduce the Shema and the prophetic exclusivity. | Single continuity marker; avoid early NT exposition. | One well-grounded transfer sentence. |
| 7. Consolidation | Explain what Isaiah adds beyond Deut. 6:4. | Brief recap and spaced retrieval prompt. | Meaningful 'Up next: Jesus and the apostles' handoff. | Own-words synthesis. |

**Rule:** No repeated “tap Next” interaction merely to create a sense of movement; each scene must advance understanding or measure the learning target. A reading-first alternative is always available.

## 6. Independent assessment bank (draft; not yet added to the app)

| ID | Standalone question | Standard evidence level | Suggested DOK | Required observable evidence |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| I1 | Identify Isaiah 43:10, 44:6, and 46:9 and state their shared central claim. | 1 | 1 | Correct reference recognition and accurate statement of exclusive identity. |
| I2 | Quote at least four of the standard's named Isaiah declarations from memory. For each, explain what its wording contributes. | 2 | 2 | Faithful KJV recall with contextual sense; distinguish before / beside / none else / call to all earth. |
| I3 | A reader sees 'one God' in 1 Corinthians 8:4. Explain how the accumulated declarations in Isaiah 43–46 inform that reading. | 3 | 3 | Explicit OT→NT link with at least two correct Isaiah passages, not merely verse enumeration. |
| I4 | Someone argues Isaiah only rejects pagan gods, not distinctions within the one divine essence. Explain that argument accurately, then present the Apostolic response using at least three Isaiah passages in context. | 4 | 3 | Charitable restatement, identifies what texts literally say, and gives coherent Oneness contextual reasoning without pretending the objection is simpler than it is. |

**Scoring:** DOK describes complexity of the task; the standard levels describe demonstrated competence. Score Scripture accuracy, context, explanation, and independence against the level requirements. Level 4 requires a coherent new defense, not merely the right multiple-choice option. Unsupported, AI-unavailable, or incomplete oral transcription responses remain **unscored**, not “partial.” Self-reported confidence is not demonstrated understanding.

**Feedback and retry:** If the learner memorizes but cannot interpret, reassemble the evidence board and require an explanatory sentence per passage. If the learner reasons well but misquotes, practice short retrieval and KJV comparison. Keep an independent retry that uses different wording. Allow continuation without falsely recording mastery; retain the response and report the distinction between completion and demonstrated mastery.

**OG.1 domain exam:** Keep the existing two recognition/connection multiple-choice items for this standard in the 10-question domain exit exam unless an owner-approved blueprint revision changes them. These items sample Levels 1–2; they do **not** certify independent Levels 3–4.

## 7. Visual and audio contract

A coherent prophetic-witness sequence: Isaiah speaking to an Israelite audience; distinct illustrative markers for a court summons and the contrast with human-made idols. Avoid depicting the LORD as a human figure, fabricating manuscript photographs, or showing an interchangeable Moses/Isaiah portrait. Scripture remains readable if imagery fails.

One stable narrator; brief optional scene introduction, user-operated Replay/Off, captions/transcript and reduced-motion mode. Support mobile, keyboard, screen readers, zoom and no-audio completion. Verify any outside video/image source and license before use.
