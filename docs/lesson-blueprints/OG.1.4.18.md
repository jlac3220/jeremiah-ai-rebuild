## 1. Authoritative learning contract

**Exact standard statement:**

> The student can clearly define the difference between Oneness monotheism and trinitarian theology, identifying the exact point of departure (the question of divine persons) and articulating why the Oneness position holds that the trinitarian formulation is not a refinement of biblical monotheism but a departure from it.

**Source scope:** The student is not expected to deliver a polemical attack on trinitarian Christians. The standard requires theological clarity: what does each position actually claim? Where do they differ? The student must be able to state the trinitarian position accurately (one substance/essence in three co-equal, co-eternal persons) before articulating how the Oneness position differs (one God who is absolutely singular, not composed of persons, who has revealed himself through roles and the incarnation). Polemics without precision fail this standard.

**Source instructional focus:** This standard requires that students can name both sides fairly. Drill them on accurately stating the trinitarian position before they refute it. A student who cannot fairly represent the opposing view has not yet earned the right to refute it.

**Exact Evidence of Learning levels** (these are *standard-defined* performance levels, not synonymous with Depth of Knowledge):

| Level | Standard label | Source evidence text |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recognition | Can state that trinitarianism teaches three persons and Oneness teaches one God. |
| 2 | Explanation | Accurately defines both positions, including the trinitarian terms substance, person, and proceeding; explains the Oneness rejection of eternal person- distinctions. |
| 3 | Application | Shows exactly where the two positions diverge exegetically (e.g., in the interpretation of Matthew 28:19, John 1:1, and the baptismal accounts in Acts) and why. |
| 4 | Defense | Engages a trinitarian challenge at a theological level — not just repeating a list of proof texts, but responding to the actual argument (e.g., "How can Jesus pray to himself?") with a theologically coherent answer grounded in the incarnation doctrine. |

## 2. Learner promise and conceptual boundaries

**Learner-facing destination:** “I can describe Trinitarian and Apostolic Oneness teaching accurately, locate their actual point of disagreement, and explain an Apostolic reading of key passages without misrepresenting those who disagree.”

**Prerequisites:** The Shema, Isaiah’s exclusivity, and NT continuity (OG.1.1–3). Learners may be unfamiliar with technical theological vocabulary; teach each term before assessing it.

**Required understandings:**
- Both views profess one God, so 'three gods versus one God' is not an accurate description of the classical disagreement.
- Classical Trinitarian theology describes one divine essence (being) in three coequal, coeternal persons; one should not confuse 'person' with three human individuals or 'essence' with a collective of gods.
- Classical formulations use language of 'proceeding' for the Spirit's eternal relation; this vocabulary must be explained accurately rather than reduced to a time-bound event.
- Apostolic Oneness theology confesses one absolutely singular God, revealed as Father, in the genuine incarnation of the Son, and as Holy Spirit; it rejects eternal personal distinctions within the Godhead.
- Jesus is fully divine and truly human in the Apostolic account. Prayer, obedience and mediation in the Gospels are real features of his human life, not performances.
- Key disputed texts require contextual handling: John 1:1, 14; Matthew 28:19 and Acts' baptismal accounts; Luke 22:42; 1 Timothy 2:5. Being able to cite them is not the same as interpreting them.

**Standard boundary:** Build the evidence needed here without absorbing the teaching and assessment obligations of adjoining standards. The **Apostolic interpretation** is the curriculum's doctrinal conclusion; competing interpretations should be represented accurately before evaluating them with Scripture. Unsupported claims must not be attributed directly to a biblical writer.

## 3. Scripture and reasoning sequence

| Order | KJV passage | Instructional contribution | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deuteronomy 6:4; Isaiah 44:6; Mark 12:29 | Re-anchor the shared confession and previous lessons. | Do not claim Trinitarians profess three gods. |
| 2 | John 1:1–14, especially 1:1 and 1:14 | Compare how each view understands the Word and the Word made flesh. | Explain the passage and the competing interpretations in their strongest recognizable form. |
| 3 | Matthew 28:18–20 and Acts 2:38; Acts 8:16; 10:48; 19:5 | Compare Jesus' baptismal command with the narrated apostolic practice. | Do not quietly equate every 'name' phrase with an identical full ritual quotation. |
| 4 | Luke 22:39–46, especially 22:42 | Read the prayer as genuine human obedience. | Do not suggest Jesus' humanity or prayers were fictitious. |
| 5 | 1 Timothy 2:3–6 | Connect the one God with the man Christ Jesus, the mediator. | Do not confuse being human with being a second or created deity. |

Required texts must be available for actual reading; generated media and optional explanations cannot replace a biblical passage.

## 4. Diagnostic misconceptions and correction

| Observed misconception | Teaching response | Independent check |
|---|---|---|
| 'Trinitarians believe in three separate gods.' | Teach the one-essence/three-person claim first; require a fair restatement before doctrinal rebuttal. | Can the learner state what a Trinitarian theologian would recognize? |
| 'Oneness means Jesus was pretending to pray.' | Read Luke 22:42 and 1 Tim 2:5; explain genuine human will, prayer, and mediation within Apostolic teaching. | Can the learner preserve genuine human experience? |
| 'Father, Son, Spirit are merely three interchangeable labels with no difference.' | Separate the one divine identity from revelation, incarnation, and the work of the Spirit; distinguish relational words in context. | Can they explain the specific scriptural terms? |
| 'One verse settles the comparison regardless of context.' | Compare John, Matthew/Acts, and Luke/Timothy; weigh contexts without a proof-text contest. | Can the learner explain why each passage matters? |
| 'Because two positions disagree, one must be treated disrespectfully.' | Model charity: define both accurately, then explain why the curriculum favors the Apostolic interpretation. | Can they respond without a caricature or personal attack? |

## 5. Lesson experience: seven different thinking tasks

| Beat | Student action | Scripture / teacher move | Proposed interaction | Evidence collected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Shared confession | Identify what both positions confess about one God. | A short recap of Deut. 6:4; state shared monotheistic profession. | Two true statements before introducing contrast. | Student distinguishes shared premise from divergence. |
| 2. Define the claims | Translate essence, person, proceeding, incarnation into plain language. | Careful short explanations and examples of misuse. | Selectable theological glossary; then learner restates each position. | Accurate definitions, not just word recognition. |
| 3. Locate the disagreement | Identify the real dispute: eternal personal distinctions versus singular divine identity in incarnation. | Introduce neutral framing followed by Apostolic thesis. | Contrast grid with 'accurate / inaccurate' classification. | Spot caricatures and replace them. |
| 4. Read John in context | Compare interpretations of the Word and the Word made flesh. | Read John 1:1–14 before offering conclusions. | Interactive passage annotations showing both readings fairly. | Independent textual explanation. |
| 5. Read Matthew, Acts, and prayer | Compare baptismal texts; then explain Jesus' real prayers and mediation. | Matthew 28; Acts 2, 8, 10, 19; Luke 22; 1 Tim 2. | Two separate inquiries, with original verses on screen. | Explains differences without conflating texts. |
| 6. Respond to a real question | Explain a Trinitarian challenge in its strongest reasonable form, then present an Apostolic answer. | Use the framework and at least two cited passages. | Written or oral response; feedback evaluates accuracy and charity. | Structured doctrinal reasoning. |
| 7. Rejoin the learning arc | Answer why correct understanding of God's identity matters for devotion. | Bridge to Deut. 6:5 without making comparison the climax. | End with Scripture identity and intentional transition to love. | One meaningful synthesis. |

**Rule:** No repeated “tap Next” interaction merely to create a sense of movement; each scene must advance understanding or measure the learning target. A reading-first alternative is always available.

## 6. Independent assessment bank (draft; not yet added to the app)

| ID | Standalone question | Standard evidence level | Suggested DOK | Required observable evidence |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| D1 | State the classical Trinitarian account of God and the Apostolic Oneness account in one or two accurate sentences each. | 1 | 1 | Names one essence/three coequal coeternal persons vs one indivisible God without eternal divine person distinctions. |
| D2 | Define essence, person, proceeding, and incarnation as they function in the two accounts. Explain why 'three gods' and 'Jesus only acted human' both misstate the positions. | 2 | 2 | Accessible but accurate terminology and two corrections with substantive reasons. |
| D3 | Read John 1:1,14 and Matthew 28:19 with Acts 2:38. Describe how the two traditions interpret these passages differently, using context rather than merely listing references. | 3 | 3 | Two clearly distinguished, fairly stated readings and a reasoned Apostolic conclusion. |
| D4 | Someone asks, 'If Jesus is God, who was he praying to?' State the strongest Trinitarian objection fairly and then explain the Apostolic response from Luke 22:42, 1 Timothy 2:5, and the genuine incarnation, without reducing prayer to an act. | 4 | 3 | No straw man, genuine humanity affirmed, coherent scripturally supported reply addressing the real challenge. |

**Scoring:** DOK describes complexity of the task; the standard levels describe demonstrated competence. Score Scripture accuracy, context, explanation, and independence against the level requirements. Level 4 requires a coherent new defense, not merely the right multiple-choice option. Unsupported, AI-unavailable, or incomplete oral transcription responses remain **unscored**, not “partial.” Self-reported confidence is not demonstrated understanding.

**Feedback and retry:** An inaccurate definition triggers a precise repair before theological argument. A one-verse answer prompts an additional contextual passage. A sarcastic or false description of Trinitarianism prompts a fair restatement and then a new attempt. Supported practice never counts as independent Level 4 evidence. Allow continuation without falsely recording mastery; retain the response and report the distinction between completion and demonstrated mastery.

**OG.1 domain exam:** Keep the existing two recognition/connection multiple-choice items for this standard in the 10-question domain exit exam unless an owner-approved blueprint revision changes them. These items sample Levels 1–2; they do **not** certify independent Levels 3–4.

## 7. Visual and audio contract

Interpretation is the focus: display actual KJV text in parallel passages, theological terms in modern restrained typography, and historically suitable scenes of Jesus praying in Gethsemane (not walking on water or a generic crucifixion). Avoid artistic pictures of separate 'God persons' presented as proof. Keep Jesus' visual continuity with earlier Gospel scenes and Isaiah distinct.

One stable narrator; brief optional scene introduction, user-operated Replay/Off, captions/transcript and reduced-motion mode. Support mobile, keyboard, screen readers, zoom and no-audio completion. Verify any outside video/image source and license before use.
