## 1. Guiding instructional idea

**Essential question:** How does Scripture's confession of the one LORD define our understanding of God, guide our reading of Jesus and the apostles, and call us to undivided worship?

**A single memorable movement:**

> Hear the confession → Hear the prophets' witness → Hear Jesus and the apostles → Explain the interpretations accurately → Respond to the one LORD.

The climax is not "winning the Trinity argument"; it is **understanding who Scripture identifies as God and why that matters for our allegiance and worship**. The doctrinal comparison is essential but forms only one chapter.

**Learner-facing promise:** By the end of OG.1 you can make a clear, scripturally supported explanation of the one LORD that connects the Old and New Testaments, fairly represents a competing interpretation, and explains why the confession leads to wholehearted love.

## 3. Transitions: do not reset the story five times

1. **Shema → Isaiah:** "Israel has confessed the LORD is one. How emphatically do the prophets describe who he is and who is not God?"
2. **Isaiah → Jesus/apostles:** "Do Jesus and those who announce him speak within this confession, or rewrite it?"
3. **Continuity → interpretation:** "If both traditions say 'one God,' where does their reading of the same Scripture actually differ?"
4. **Interpretation → devotion:** "Once we know what Scripture says about God's identity, what does Deuteronomy immediately require of those who hear?"
5. **Devotion → conclusion:** "Explain one coherent truth: who the LORD is, how Scripture witnesses to him, and why that matters for faithful response."

Only the opening needs a distinct introduction to the full journey. After it, a lightweight contents view should let the learner see five chapters, their place, and a truthful "Up next." Allow review of prior chapters; do not conflate *viewed* with *mastered*.

## 4. Teaching model per standard

- **Encounter** a genuine text or contextual historical event before an extended interpretation.
- **Observe** a bounded question the learner can answer from that text.
- **Teach** the needed meaning explicitly, one decisive idea at a time.
- **Investigate** through an activity that suits the cognitive goal (evidence map, context comparison, claim clinic, application).
- **Practice** with actionable corrective feedback (not just Correct / Incorrect).
- **Explain or transfer independently**; collect evidence against the standard-specific rubric.
- **Close** with one durable takeaway and a bridge, not a new unrelated doctrine.

This is a functional arc, **not** a rigid seven-slide formula. Some moves combine beats; narration/imagery are optional whenever they would impede reading.

## 5. Assessment design: meaningful evidence, not more quizzes

### Existing OG.1 exam stays intact until separately approved

The current 10-item multiple-choice exit exam has **two items per standard**. Preserve that exam and its answer history. It samples recognition and lower-level connections; it does not, by itself, certify level-3 interpretation or level-4 defense.

### Proposed independent performance evidence

The draft blueprints contain **four candidate assessment tasks per standard** (20 candidates total), one targeting each standard-defined evidence level. **Do not deliver all 20 as a mandatory second exam.** The bank is a design resource; choose a lean, adaptive evidence plan that collects required evidence across the journey without duplication.

| Standard | One strong independent task candidate | Why the MC exam alone is insufficient |
|---|---|---|
| OG.1.1.18 | S4: Evaluate Genesis 2:24's "one flesh" analogy using the Shema in context | Recall of “one LORD” cannot show contextual defense |
| OG.1.2.18 | I4: Answer a contextual Isaiah exclusivity challenge using multiple passages | Identifying a verse cannot show cumulative prophetic reasoning |
| OG.1.3.18 | N4: Interpret Paul's Father/Lord language within Shema monotheism | Selecting Paul's words cannot show OT→NT argument |
| OG.1.4.18 | D4: Explain Jesus' prayers via genuine incarnation after accurately framing the opposing claim | Defining the Trinity alone cannot demonstrate a reasoned response |
| OG.1.5.18 | E4: Explain how one-LORD doctrine yields lived allegiance while distinguishing observable work from sincerity | Recall of v. 5 cannot establish grounded application |

**Assessment provenance:** Store `standardId`, blueprint version, response text or consented oral transcript, rubric criteria, the source of assessment (objective key / verified meaning-based evaluation / service unavailable), support usage, feedback, time, and attempts. Independent level-3/4 credit requires substantive reasoning and verified grading. Never use keyword-matching or a provider outage to fabricate understanding.

**Level 1–4:** Respect each standard's exact evidence requirements. The same numerical level means the *kind* of evidence demonstrated, not a raw percentage converted into a theological judgment. DOK 1–4 is a separate descriptor of task complexity. Report missing or unscored work as missing evidence, not zero and not partial proficiency.

**Completion versus mastery:** A learner may finish the journey and see a complete experience even if some assessments are unscored or below target. The report must state what was actually observed and provide a revisit/retry path. Do not secretly advance a mastery claim.

**Proposed minimal path (for review):** small embedded objective checks as teaching feedback; one independent explanation in each chapter (five total), with an adaptive follow-up challenge when needed; existing ten-item domain exit exam; per-standard understanding report; optional ungraded reflection and survey. Do not force an unrelated 20-question written battery.

## 6. Answer quality and fairness rubric

Across the five standards, evaluate the actual authored criteria:

| Dimension | Strong evidence | Insufficient evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Scripture accuracy** | Correct passage wording/reference and faithful contextual use | Incorrect attribution, unsourced assertion or quoted words not in text |
| **Explanation** | Demonstrates why the passage supports the claim | Repeats a slogan or choices without interpreting them |
| **Connection** | Links verses without losing either context | Uses a list of citations as if it were an argument |
| **Fairness** | Describes Trinitarian claims in language a proponent could recognize before Apostolic critique | “Trinitarians worship three gods” caricature |
| **Transfer** | Applies the reasoning to a fresh passage or argument without hints | Copies sample explanation |
| **Independence** | Reasoning given without answer-revealing scaffolds | Supported practice misreported as independent mastery |

Feedback should name the relevant missing criterion and offer instruction. It should never be merely a disapproving label. The system does not assess a person's sincerity, standing with God, or spiritual feelings.

## 7. Media direction: sensory, specific, purposeful

**Biblical scenes:** Distinct depictions for ancient Israel hearing Deuteronomy, Isaiah's prophetic setting, Jesus speaking with a scribe, Paul addressing believers, and Jesus praying where relevant. No interchangeable actor portraits, ahistorical manuscript claims, or generic "Bible person" repeatedly reused.

**Modern lived scenes:** Diverse everyday people in authentic situations of care, worship, and responsibility for OG.1.5; avoid repeated laptop stock imagery. No image should pretend to prove an argument or judge a person's holiness.

**Narration:** One narrator (currently Ash); short optional first-visit audio, clear Replay/Off, no voice picker, no sound required for progress. The player and narration service must share an exact authored-script contract.

**Accessibility:** Readable KJV primary, inline passage context, keyboard and screen-reader controls, large touch targets, reduced motion, captions/transcripts, and reliable behavior on low bandwidth.

**Source verification:** Media URLs/licenses, caption claims and authenticity need review; do not silently place an unaudited external asset on the live app.

### Definition of done

- Each authoritative standard requirement is taught and linked to at least one demonstrable assessment.
- The student can explain reasoning, not only select answers.
- Five chapters feel like one purposeful journey, not repeated quiz cards.
- No mastery on unavailable AI assessments; attempts and restart history survive.
- The Scripture text and narrator agree, and no lesson requires audio.
- A user can identify chapter status, results, limits of the scoring, and an appropriate next lesson.
- Accessible mobile experience and end-of-lesson survey work as tested.
