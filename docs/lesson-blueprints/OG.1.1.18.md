## 1. Authoritative standard contract

**Exact statement (source):**
> The student can recite, translate, and expound Deuteronomy 6:4 as the irreducible confession of biblical monotheism, demonstrating how it functions as the interpretive framework for all subsequent revelation about God.

The source **scope** requires more than a memorized statement: learners must explain the Shema's continuing significance, Jesus' reaffirmation in Mark 12:29, and why Apostolic Oneness teaching interprets later revelation within the confession of one God. The source **focus** calls for an authoritative confession, not just a slogan, with Isaiah used to reinforce exclusivity and other theological claims compared accurately.

**Exact evidence expectations, condensed from the standard:**

| Standard level | Source label | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recognition | States the Shema and its basic meaning |
| 2 | Explanation | Explains `echad` as “one” in its covenant setting and why this text confesses the oneness of God rather than a claim about compound divine persons |
| 3 | Application | Traces how Mark 12:29, 1 Corinthians 8:4–6, and Ephesians 4:5–6 continue Shema monotheism in the Apostolic reading |
| 4 | Defense | Reasons through a proposed “compound unity” reading using Genesis 2:24, the context of the Shema, and Isaiah's declarations of exclusivity |

These are **standard evidence levels**, not automatic synonyms for Webb DOK levels.

**Doctrinal and rhetorical boundary:** The lesson teaches the Apostolic/Oneness interpretation confidently. When representing classical Trinitarianism, describe it fairly as **one divine essence in three coequal, coeternal persons**, not as three separate gods. A detailed comparison is central to OG.1.4.18 and should not consume this standard's primary instruction.

## 2. Learner destination

**Student-facing promise:** “I can explain what Israel confessed in Deuteronomy 6:4, why the words matter in their context, and why this remains the starting point when I read what Jesus and the apostles teach about God.”

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with locating a Bible reference. No prior Hebrew or theological vocabulary assumed. The adult endpoint (.18) requires independent explanation; younger adaptations change support and pacing, never the underlying truth.

**Nonnegotiable knowledge/skills:**
- The Shema is Deuteronomy 6:4; the KJV wording and central confession of **one LORD** are known.
- `shema` opens with a summons to hear/listen; `echad` is commonly rendered **one**. Context determines the assertion about the LORD; a stand-alone vocabulary claim does not resolve all later doctrinal questions.
- Deuteronomy 6:4–5 connects divine identity with covenant allegiance and wholehearted love (full ethical application follows in OG.1.5.18).
- Isaiah 44:6 and 45:5 reinforce that the LORD does not have another God beside him (full Isaiah treatment follows in OG.1.2.18).
- Jesus repeats the confession in Mark 12:29; later apostolic references must be read with continuity in view (full NT treatment follows in OG.1.3.18).

**Transfer target:** Given an unfamiliar statement claiming that “one” *must* mean composite unity, the learner can explain why the semantic possibility of the word alone is insufficient, and build a concise response using Scripture in context.

## 3. Scripture and reasoning map

| Order | Passage (KJV) | Instructional role | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deuteronomy 6:4 | Begin with the confession: “Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD.” | Read the verse before presenting a doctrinal label |
| 2 | Deuteronomy 6:5 and surrounding covenant setting | Show what the confession calls Israel to do | Do not turn this lesson into OG.1.5's full ethical treatment |
| 3 | Isaiah 44:6; 45:5 | Follow Scripture's own exclusion of another God beside the LORD | Reinforcement here, detailed exposition in OG.1.2 |
| 4 | Mark 12:28–29 | Hear Jesus repeat the Shema | Do not imply Jesus invented a new confession |
| 5 | 1 Corinthians 8:4–6; Ephesians 4:5–6 | Preview the apostolic continuity | Save extended treatment for OG.1.3 |
| 6 | Genesis 2:24 (for the later reasoning challenge) | Compare another use of “one” without collapsing unlike contexts | A human “one flesh” analogy does not independently establish the metaphysical identity of God |

**Primary textual authority:** Scripture in the KJV. External explanatory videos, Hebrew notes, historical manuscripts, and generated imagery are optional enrichment—not the basis of the doctrinal claim.

## 4. Misconceptions we will actually diagnose

| Learner statement | Response / next teaching move |
|---|---|
| “One God” is a slogan I cannot explain. | Re-read Deut. 6:4, then have learner explain the *claim* and *referent* in their own words. |
| The word `echad` always means a composite entity. | Clarify basic translation as “one”; examine the noun, grammar, context, and Isaiah's corroboration rather than assuming a universal lexical rule. |
| The word `echad` can *never* be used in contexts of composite things. | Do not teach a false lexical absolute. Distinguish how a word functions in Genesis 2:24 from what Deut. 6:4 asserts about the LORD. |
| Jesus or the apostles replaced Israel's confession. | Read Mark 12:29 aloud, then compare Paul's language in its context. |
| Trinitarians simply believe in three separate gods. | Correct the caricature accurately before describing the Apostolic disagreement. |
| I can repeat the right answer, so I must have mastered the standard. | Ask for a new scriptural explanation and an independent response to a novel claim. |

## 5. Learning journey (a storyboard of *thinking*, not repetitive slides)

| Beat | Learner does | Teacher / Scripture contributes | Proposed experience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Encounter** | Hears or reads Deut. 6:4 without receiving the conclusion first | Brief context: Israel is addressed and summoned to hear | A historically plausible communal listening moment with quiet voice/text controls; optional verified Shema/Listen video |
| **2. Observe** | Identifies who speaks, who is addressed, and what is confessed | Teaches `shema`, `LORD`, and `echad` only as needed for the passage | Tap-to-reveal meaningful text annotations; no trivia game |
| **3. Explain** | Restates the confession using their own words | Connects one LORD to covenant setting and v. 5 | Short open response, then targeted feedback; no answer revealed in the prompt |
| **4. Strengthen** | Compares Deut. 6:4 with Isaiah's “beside me” statements | Introduces the force of exclusion without treating Isaiah as a disconnected proof-text list | Two-passage comparison with contextual Biblical scene changes (not reused person cutouts) |
| **5. Follow through** | Notices what Jesus says about the confession in Mark 12:29 | Shows OT → Jesus continuity; briefly previews apostolic language | Text-first comparison and optional narrator; uses learner's earlier observation |
| **6. Reason** | Evaluates the Genesis 2:24 / `echad` argument and supports an answer with Scripture | Models charitable description and contextual reasoning before asking for a fresh attempt | Choose an argument to inspect, then give an *independent* explanation—not a loaded multiple choice |
| **7. Consolidate** | Summarizes one truth, one passage, and why it matters for reading the rest of Scripture | Connects forward to OG.1.2 and later standards | Simple personalized recap, short retrieval check, “Up next: Isaiah” |

The amount of motion, imagery, and audio is determined by the teaching task. Never let media interrupt or replace Scripture reading. Include a quieter reading mode and a meaningful change of activity throughout the sequence.

## 6. Assessment blueprint

**Diagnostic (ungraded):** “Before we begin, what do you think Deuteronomy 6:4 is claiming?” This customizes emphasis but does **not** skip the core teaching.

**Formative evidence:**
- Rephrase Deut. 6:4 in plain language after reading it.
- Identify which words in Isaiah reinforce the confession and explain their significance.
- Describe what Jesus quotes in Mark 12:29 and what that contributes to continuity.

**Proposed independent assessment tasks** (draft; not yet approved or added to the application):

| ID | Prompt | Standard level | Suggested DOK | What counts as evidence |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| S1 | “Without looking at the passage, recite or write Deuteronomy 6:4 from memory, then identify who is being addressed.” | 1 | 1 | Faithful recall of the KJV confession (allowing inconsequential punctuation or speech-transcription variation), including one LORD and Israel as addressee |
| S2 | “What does `echad` contribute to Deuteronomy 6:4? Explain why the meaning of a word must be read within the sentence and surrounding passage.” | 2 | 2 | “One”; identifies the referent, covenant context, and interprets without ungrounded Hebrew absolutes |
| S3 | “Explain how Mark 12:29 connects to Deuteronomy 6:4, then use one apostolic text to show continuity in the confession of God.” | 3 | 3 | Correct Jesus / OT link, accurate text support, defensible interpretation rather than citation alone |
| S4 | “A reader argues that because Genesis 2:24 says two people become ‘one flesh,’ Deuteronomy 6:4 may also describe several divine persons. Evaluate the comparison and explain your conclusion with Scripture.” | 4 | 3 | Fairly describes analogy, distinguishes referents/contexts, uses Deut. 6:4 and Isaiah to build coherent Apostolic response |

**Recitation contract:** The standard explicitly requires recitation, translation, and exposition. Collect an independent memory recall in addition to the `echad` explanation. Either typed recall or consented, reviewed oral transcription can supply evidence; do not confuse audio-availability or minor transcription errors with doctrinal understanding. The app must make the original KJV text available for instruction and practice, but not reveal it during independent memory recall.

**Proposed scoring guidance:** Track correct Scripture use, explanatory accuracy, contextual reasoning, and independence separately. Evidence levels describe **demonstrated** work, not a percent score, number of taps, or how confidently the narrator sounds. Recognition-only evidence must never be reported as level 4. Provide corrective feedback and alternate practice if the learner does not reach the target; keep their progress and permit retry.

**Existing assessment gap to review:** The current OG.1 domain exit exam has **two multiple-choice items for OG.1.1.18** (Shema confession and hearing). These sample introductory understanding, but on their own do not prove independent application or level-4 defense. Preserve the existing exam while discussing how to add performance evidence without making the learner answer repetitive questions. The **ten-question OG.1 domain exit exam** spans five standards; it is not a ten-question test for this one standard.

## 7. Experience, narration, and accessibility

- **Historical imagery:** For the opening, depict Israel hearing the covenant confession; when comparing prophetic texts, show Isaiah in a context appropriate to the passage. Do not reuse Moses/Isaiah/Jesus as an interchangeable generic biblical figure; avoid fabricated archaeology.
- **Visual hierarchy:** Scripture readable first; simple, purposeful annotations second; immersive scene when it adds meaning; no unrelated decorative images.
- **Narration:** One stable voice, short optional automatic intros, user-controlled mute/replay, accurate verse pronunciation and captions. Do not require sound to complete the standard.
- **Inclusion:** Keyboard navigation, screen-reader names, alt text, reduced-motion support, contrast, scalable type, mobile ergonomics, captions/transcripts, no time pressure.
- **Source discipline:** Verify any outside link, rights and factual claims; use existing BibleProject Shema reference only as optional language/context enrichment.
