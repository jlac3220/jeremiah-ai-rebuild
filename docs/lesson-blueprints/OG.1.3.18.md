## 1. Authoritative learning contract

**Exact standard statement:**

> The student demonstrates that the New Testament apostolic writers operate within, not beyond, Old Testament monotheism — and can show from 1 Corinthians 8:4–6, Ephesians 4:5–6, 1 Timothy 2:5, and James 2:19 that the apostolic confession of God's oneness is identical in kind to the Shema, not a revision of it.

**Source scope:** This standard addresses a common confusion: that the New Testament introduces a more complex Godhead. The student must be able to demonstrate that every apostolic statement about the oneness of God is consistent with — and draws directly from — the Old Testament monotheistic tradition. The phrase "one God, the Father" (1 Cor. 8:6) must be interpreted within Jewish monotheism, not read as introducing a distinct person named "Father" within a trinity.

**Source instructional focus:** Help students see the Bible as a single unfolding monotheistic story. The apostles were Jews who never stopped being monotheists. Their language about Jesus must be interpreted within that framework, not as a departure from it.

**Exact Evidence of Learning levels** (these are *standard-defined* performance levels, not synonymous with Depth of Knowledge):

| Level | Standard label | Source evidence text |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recognition | Identifies the NT monotheistic texts and their basic claim. |
| 2 | Explanation | Shows how each text depends on Old Testament monotheism rather than revising it; explains why Paul's "one God, the Father" is not a trinitarian formula. |
| 3 | Application | Constructs a brief argument showing OT → NT continuity of monotheistic confession as a throughline from Deuteronomy through Jesus's own words to Paul and James. |
| 4 | Defense | Responds to the claim that 1 Corinthians 8:6 introduces a binitarian or trinitarian structure, showing that Paul is distinguishing the true God from pagan deities — not introducing internal divine persons. |

## 2. Learner promise and conceptual boundaries

**Learner-facing destination:** “I can trace the confession of one God from Moses to Jesus and the apostles and explain why the Apostolic interpretation understands the New Testament as continuity, not replacement.”

**Prerequisites:** OG.1.1.18 and the core Isaiah witness of OG.1.2.18. Introduce 1 Corinthians 8 before quoting v. 6 in isolation.

**Required understandings:**
- Jesus quotes the Shema in Mark 12:28–29 when answering about the first commandment.
- 1 Corinthians 8:4–6 addresses idols and one God / one Lord in the setting of a Christian community.
- Ephesians 4:4–6 places one God within a connected confession of unity; 1 Timothy 2:5 affirms one God and a genuinely human mediator.
- James 2:19 treats God's oneness as recognizable but shows bare assent is not the same as a faithful response.
- The Apostolic/Oneness interpretation reads these texts within the foundational confession; alternative readings of 'one God, the Father' and 'one Lord Jesus Christ' must be represented fairly before critique.

**Standard boundary:** Build the evidence needed here without absorbing the teaching and assessment obligations of adjoining standards. The **Apostolic interpretation** is the curriculum's doctrinal conclusion; competing interpretations should be represented accurately before evaluating them with Scripture. Unsupported claims must not be attributed directly to a biblical writer.

## 3. Scripture and reasoning sequence

| Order | KJV passage | Instructional contribution | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deuteronomy 6:4; Isaiah 44:6 | Recall the Old Testament claim first. | Brief retrieval only; do not reteach two previous chapters. |
| 2 | Mark 12:28–34, especially 12:29 | Read Jesus' question/answer as an actual exchange. | Keep the first commandment's setting. |
| 3 | 1 Corinthians 8:1–6 | Read the idol-food background and precise contrast of so-called gods with the Christian confession. | Do not omit the challenging 'one God, the Father ... one Lord, Jesus Christ' wording. |
| 4 | Ephesians 4:4–6 | Trace connected one-language across Spirit, Lord, faith, baptism, God and Father. | Do not equate each enumerated 'one' with an identical referent. |
| 5 | 1 Timothy 2:3–6 | Link the one God with the man Christ Jesus as mediator. | Honor the incarnation and genuine humanity. |
| 6 | James 2:14–20, especially 2:19 | Distinguish belief in one God from living response. | Detailed devotion follows in OG.1.5.18. |

Required texts must be available for actual reading; generated media and optional explanations cannot replace a biblical passage.

## 4. Diagnostic misconceptions and correction

| Observed misconception | Teaching response | Independent check |
|---|---|---|
| 'The New Testament discarded the Shema.' | Have the learner identify what Jesus actually quotes in Mark 12:29. | Can they quote the shared text and context? |
| 'Paul's phrasing can be explained by counting the words Father and Lord.' | Read 1 Corinthians 8:1–6; examine the idols contrast and the person/work terms in context. | Can they reason from the passage, not labels alone? |
| 'Jesus being a mediator means he is not truly human.' | Read 1 Timothy 2:5's actual 'man Christ Jesus' wording. | Can they explain the text without denying real humanity? |
| 'James 2:19 says correct belief is irrelevant.' | Distinguish necessary confession from insufficient assent; read James' context. | Can they articulate the contrast without dismissing doctrine? |

## 5. Lesson experience: seven different thinking tasks

| Beat | Student action | Scripture / teacher move | Proposed interaction | Evidence collected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Anchor retrieval | Recall the exact claim of Deut. 6:4 and Isaiah 44:6. | A one-minute retrieval, then show KJV. | Small passage cards, not a restart of OG.1. | Prior-knowledge signal. |
| 2. Jesus answers | Notice Jesus' choice of Scripture in Mark 12. | Read 12:28–34; ask what he affirms. | Gospel scene: Jesus teaching a scribe; no generic crucifixion art. | Identification and explanation. |
| 3. Paul's challenge | Read Corinthians in its argument about idols. | 1 Corinthians 8:1–6 with 'so-called gods' context. | Two-column comparison: reported idols / Paul's confession. | Context-sensitive explanation. |
| 4. Follow the apostles | Identify contribution of Ephesians, 1 Timothy, James. | Read three short linked passages, not a verse dump. | Timed-by-learner continuity path Deut → Mark → Paul → wider witness. | Passage-specific contribution. |
| 5. Interpret the difficult wording | Explain 'Father' and 'Lord' in context fairly. | Present the Apostolic reading and outline the alternative Trinitarian reading without mislabeling it. | Expandable side-by-side context, no automatic agreement button. | Independent context-based interpretation. |
| 6. Transfer | Respond to 'Paul revises the Shema into two gods/two divine persons.' | Ask for a coherent argument using at least three distinct passages. | Short written or oral explanation; balanced feedback. | Application evidence. |
| 7. Bridge | State the question that remains for OG.1.4. | Introduce doctrine-comparison question, not its complete answer. | Up-next screen on real interpretive difference. | Accurate question phrasing. |

**Rule:** No repeated “tap Next” interaction merely to create a sense of movement; each scene must advance understanding or measure the learning target. A reading-first alternative is always available.

## 6. Independent assessment bank (draft; not yet added to the app)

| ID | Standalone question | Standard evidence level | Suggested DOK | Required observable evidence |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| N1 | Identify the Old Testament confession Jesus quotes in Mark 12:29 and the issue Jesus is answering. | 1 | 1 | Correct passage and meaning; speaker/context known. |
| N2 | Explain how 1 Corinthians 8:4–6 and Ephesians 4:4–6 use the language of one God in their own contexts. | 2 | 2 | Correct idol contrast, community unity, and textual specifics. |
| N3 | Construct a short Deuteronomy → Mark → Corinthians → James explanation of why the Apostolic confession is continuous. | 3 | 3 | At least three accurate linked passages, clear logical transitions, no unsupported quotation. |
| N4 | A reader says Paul's 'one God, the Father' and 'one Lord Jesus Christ' revise the Shema. Give the strongest fair reason that reader might offer, then develop the Apostolic reply using Paul's context, Jesus' reaffirmation, and the incarnation. | 4 | 3 | Correctly states the other reading and gives coherent response grounded in the cited texts and genuine human mediation. |

**Scoring:** DOK describes complexity of the task; the standard levels describe demonstrated competence. Score Scripture accuracy, context, explanation, and independence against the level requirements. Level 4 requires a coherent new defense, not merely the right multiple-choice option. Unsupported, AI-unavailable, or incomplete oral transcription responses remain **unscored**, not “partial.” Self-reported confidence is not demonstrated understanding.

**Feedback and retry:** For verse-list-only responses, return to the complete argument of 1 Corinthians 8. For reductive comparisons, require a fair restatement of the alternate view before critique. Learner receives a different passage-order transfer after feedback. Allow continuation without falsely recording mastery; retain the response and report the distinction between completion and demonstrated mastery.

**OG.1 domain exam:** Keep the existing two recognition/connection multiple-choice items for this standard in the 10-question domain exit exam unless an owner-approved blueprint revision changes them. These items sample Levels 1–2; they do **not** certify independent Levels 3–4.

## 7. Visual and audio contract

Specific passage scenes: Jesus responding to a scribe in the setting of Mark 12; Paul teaching or writing to a diverse first-century church, not anachronistic modern clothing; clearly distinct figures. Scripture-trail graphics can be modern and neutral. Do not imply an image is a historical photograph.

One stable narrator; brief optional scene introduction, user-operated Replay/Off, captions/transcript and reduced-motion mode. Support mobile, keyboard, screen readers, zoom and no-audio completion. Verify any outside video/image source and license before use.
