## 1. Authoritative learning contract

**Exact standard statement:**

> The student articulates how the confession of God's absolute oneness is not merely intellectual but doxological and ethical — producing exclusive devotion, covenantal faithfulness, and a worldview in which all things are held together by one sovereign Lord.

**Source scope:** Deuteronomy 6:4 is immediately followed by the Shema's application: "love the LORD thy God with all thine heart" (v.5). The confession of divine unity produces exclusive devotion because a divided God could permit divided loyalty. This standard asks students to connect doctrine to life — the oneness of God is not a theological curiosity but the foundation of worship, ethics, mission, and identity. A student who can argue monotheism philosophically but does not feel its devotional weight has not fully met this standard.

**Source instructional focus:** End this domain by grounding it in worship and love. Students who have been trained in this doctrine since childhood can become argumentatively skilled while remaining personally cold. This standard calls for integration of head and heart.

**Exact Evidence of Learning levels** (these are *standard-defined* performance levels, not synonymous with Depth of Knowledge):

| Level | Standard label | Source evidence text |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recognition | States that the Shema has a commandment attached (love God completely). |
| 2 | Explanation | Explains the logical connection between numerical divine unity and the demand for undivided human devotion; can relate this to the First and Second Commandments. |
| 3 | Application | Articulates how the oneness of God shapes apostolic mission (one gospel, one salvation, one Lord), worship (exclusive devotion to Jesus), and ethics (no divided moral allegiances). |
| 4 | Defense | Responds to the charge that Oneness theology is merely an intellectual position by demonstrating its generative power for spiritual life, evangelism, and community — showing that knowing this God changes everything. |

## 2. Learner promise and conceptual boundaries

**Learner-facing destination:** “I can explain why the confession of one LORD calls for wholehearted love and exclusive devotion, and apply that connection to worship, moral allegiance, mission, and life with other people.”

**Prerequisites:** Learner has encountered the Shema, Isaiah's witness, apostolic continuity, and the doctrinal comparison. Do not assume theology vocabulary proves spiritual maturity.

**Required understandings:**
- Deuteronomy 6:4–5 moves directly from who the LORD is to a command to love him with heart, soul, and might.
- Exodus 20:3–5 guards against worship of rival gods and images; one divine authority and divided allegiance do not coexist in Israel's command.
- James 2:19 distinguishes true recognition of one God from the larger demand that faith have living consequences.
- Apostolic teaching connects one Lord with one gospel, worship, community, and ethical life; the student should articulate the connection rather than invent new moral rules from these verses.
- The app can assess interpretation and application reasoning, but **cannot** grade sincerity, affection for God, private holiness, or spiritual standing. Do not infer spiritual condition from a survey answer, completion or audio behavior.

**Standard boundary:** Build the evidence needed here without absorbing the teaching and assessment obligations of adjoining standards. The **Apostolic interpretation** is the curriculum's doctrinal conclusion; competing interpretations should be represented accurately before evaluating them with Scripture. Unsupported claims must not be attributed directly to a biblical writer.

## 3. Scripture and reasoning sequence

| Order | KJV passage | Instructional contribution | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deuteronomy 6:4–9, especially 6:4–5 | Make the connection between the one LORD and wholehearted love explicit. | Keep covenant-life context: teach, remember, speak, live. |
| 2 | Exodus 20:1–6, especially 20:3–5 | Compare exclusive devotion to the first commandments. | Avoid attributing later ethical codes to this passage. |
| 3 | Mark 12:28–34 | See Jesus keep confession and love together. | Stay within the teaching purpose; detailed historical continuity is earlier OG.1.3. |
| 4 | James 2:14–20, especially 2:19 | Contrast mere belief that God is one with lived faith. | Do not claim James says knowledge or confession is irrelevant. |
| 5 | Ephesians 4:4–6 and 1 Corinthians 8:4–6 | Trace unity, one Lord and a community formed by the confession. | Detailed exegetical dispute belongs to OG.1.3. |
| 6 | Acts 2:36–42 (supporting) | Illustrate how apostolic proclamation leads into communal life. | Do not duplicate the full NB New Birth track or reframe this as a salvation assessment. |

Required texts must be available for actual reading; generated media and optional explanations cannot replace a biblical passage.

## 4. Diagnostic misconceptions and correction

| Observed misconception | Teaching response | Independent check |
|---|---|---|
| 'Oneness is only an argument I must win.' | Show the imperative immediately following the confession (Deut. 6:5), then ask what kind of allegiance follows. | Can the learner connect identity, command, and human response? |
| 'Exclusive devotion means only having strong feelings.' | Read love in Deut 6:4–9 and the commandments, distinguishing emotion from faithful, whole-life allegiance. | Can they explain behavior without inventing a checklist? |
| 'A correct doctrinal answer proves someone loves God.' | Read James 2:19 in context; explain what can and cannot be inferred. | Can the learner distinguish observable explanation from inaccessible sincerity? |
| 'One gospel means winning every doctrinal disagreement.' | Model mission as faithful witness and care for people, not combative debate. | Can they link truth to respectful proclamation and community life? |

## 5. Lesson experience: seven different thinking tasks

| Beat | Student action | Scripture / teacher move | Proposed interaction | Evidence collected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Return to the sentence | Recall Deut. 6:4, then immediately read v. 5. | 'Who is the LORD?' and 'What does he command?' remain together. | Verses 4 and 5 revealed as one scriptural passage, not a surprise moral lesson. | Student identifies the connective movement. |
| 2. Meaning of undivided | Trace love with heart, soul, might into covenant life. | Deut. 6:5–9; short KJV reading in context. | Learner explores three dimensions and then summarizes; avoid forced emotion. | Explains command in own words. |
| 3. Exclusive worship | Explain why Exodus 20 excludes rival gods and images. | Exodus 20:1–6 paired with Deut. 6:4–5. | Compare command and underlying identity, not a true/false quiz. | Correct scriptural reasoning. |
| 4. Assent versus response | Notice James 2:19's challenge. | Read James in the wider paragraph. | Short misconception sorting: what belief shows / cannot show. | Interpretation with necessary nuance. |
| 5. Transfer to practice | Examine a realistic, non-coercive scenario about conflicting loyalties, witness, or community care. | Show a case without demanding private confessions or invented moral laws. | Choose a response, defend the Scripture-to-action connection. | Independent application and respect. |
| 6. Explain what changes | Summarize doctrinal identity → worship → ethical allegiance → mission. | Return to one Lord through Jesus and apostolic witness. | Visual loop from Deut 6:4–5 to today; text-only option. | Synthesis grounded in sources. |
| 7. Close the whole OG.1 journey | State how knowing God's identity reshapes the kind of devotion he commands. | Quiet, purposeful closing; separate academic rubric from personal worship. | Personal reflection optional and private, then independent exit evaluation. | Thoughtful summary, not a graded spiritual profession. |

**Rule:** No repeated “tap Next” interaction merely to create a sense of movement; each scene must advance understanding or measure the learning target. A reading-first alternative is always available.

## 6. Independent assessment bank (draft; not yet added to the app)

| ID | Standalone question | Standard evidence level | Suggested DOK | Required observable evidence |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| E1 | According to Deuteronomy 6:4–5, what command immediately follows the confession of the one LORD? | 1 | 1 | Accurate command to love the LORD wholeheartedly. |
| E2 | Explain the relationship between Deuteronomy 6:4–5 and the first two commandments in Exodus 20:3–5. | 2 | 2 | Connects the one God with exclusive worship, rejects rival allegiances, does not just repeat texts. |
| E3 | A believer understands the doctrine but treats worship and community obligations as unrelated. Explain the link from the Shema to worship, moral allegiance, and apostolic mission. | 3 | 3 | At least two scriptural connections plus a credible explanation in the student's own words. |
| E4 | A critic claims Oneness is only intellectual argument. Respond using Deut 6:4–9, James 2:19, and an example involving community or witness. Distinguish what a teacher can assess from what only God knows about a person's heart. | 4 | 3 | Doctrinal application, fair response, concrete noncoercive example, and explicit humility about unobservable devotion. |

**Scoring:** DOK describes complexity of the task; the standard levels describe demonstrated competence. Score Scripture accuracy, context, explanation, and independence against the level requirements. Level 4 requires a coherent new defense, not merely the right multiple-choice option. Unsupported, AI-unavailable, or incomplete oral transcription responses remain **unscored**, not “partial.” Self-reported confidence is not demonstrated understanding.

**Feedback and retry:** If a learner only repeats 'love God,' ask them to connect a specific command and a fresh lived example. If they give an arbitrary moral rule, return to the passages and scope. Spiritual sincerity is never a scored variable. Offer ungraded reflection and alternative contexts. Allow continuation without falsely recording mastery; retain the response and report the distinction between completion and demonstrated mastery.

**OG.1 domain exam:** Keep the existing two recognition/connection multiple-choice items for this standard in the 10-question domain exit exam unless an owner-approved blueprint revision changes them. These items sample Levels 1–2; they do **not** certify independent Levels 3–4.

## 7. Visual and audio contract

Return to the opening confession but change the setting: one rich historical Deuteronomy scene followed by neutral, respectful modern settings of shared worship, family responsibility, neighborly care, and community service. Avoid showing the same anonymous person at a laptop repeatedly. No image should imply an individual is spiritually inferior based on dress, culture, or visible traits.

One stable narrator; brief optional scene introduction, user-operated Replay/Off, captions/transcript and reduced-motion mode. Support mobile, keyboard, screen readers, zoom and no-audio completion. Verify any outside video/image source and license before use.
